0. Motivation

We were deeply struck by the fact that pressure ulcers — a condition experienced by many immobile patients — can lead to a mortality rate as high as 70%, despite being preventable. Prevention largely depends on two key actions: regular postural changes and maintaining an appropriate environment. However, doing so manually places a heavy burden on caregivers and medical staff. Motivated by this, we set out to develop an automated pressure ulcer prevention system that can monitor and manage risk factors without constant human intervention.

    • 🔍 Technical Details

    ▲ Overall System Architecture


    1️⃣ IoT Logic (ESP32 + MQTT)

    • The ESP32 module collects data every 5 minutes from the DS18B20, DHT22, and RFID-RC522 sensors.
    • These sensor values are formatted as JSON and transmitted to both the MQTT broker (HiveMQ) and Firebase Realtime Database.
    • The MQTT broker instantly forwards the real-time data to the iOS app, enabling live monitoring.
    • The data stored in Firebase is later sent to the backend server for logging and further analysis.

    • Why choose ESP32 over Arduino?
      ESP32 was chosen over a traditional Arduino due to its built-in Wi-Fi capabilities, lower power consumption, and suitability for wireless IoT communication in long-term deployments.

    2️⃣ Machine Learning (Random Forest)

    \[ \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=t-M}^{t} |\Delta T_i| < \varepsilon, \quad \text{with } M=5,\ \varepsilon=0.1^\circ C \]

    • A Random Forest model was trained using sensor-derived features, including recent temperature stability patterns, to predict the optimal timing for the next posture change.
    • The concept of "near-high-temperature stability" was used to identify sustained pressure risk conditions based on minimal temperature variation across recent intervals.
    • Model inference is executed in the backend (FastAPI) whenever a new posture is tagged.

    • Why Random Forest?
      To provide personalized predictions for a diverse patient population, we explored alternative models such as LightGBM. However, it exhibited inferior performance in our evaluation compared to Random Forest. Random Forest was ultimately selected due to its strong performance on small-to-medium-sized datasets, robustness to noise, and ability to model non-linear interactions across multiple sensor inputs while minimizing overfitting through its ensemble structure.

    3️⃣ Daily Summary via LLM

    • When a patient transitions to sleep mode, the backend collects sensor logs from the past 24 hours and constructs a Korean-language prompt for GPT-4 generation.
    • The prompt includes not only today's air and cushion temperature patterns, posture changes, and patient metadata, but also comparative trends from the previous week.
    • GPT-4 generates a daily summary in fluent Korean, integrating medical insights, behavioral patterns, and suggested actions to improve the patient's condition.
    • The result is displayed in natural language within the iOS caregiver app, helping caregivers make informed decisions without interpreting raw sensor data.
    • Each summary includes personalized guidance such as recommended posture change timing or environmental adjustments (e.g., cushion temperature), enhancing proactive care.
    • Summaries are generated automatically at sleep transition and optionally stored in Firebase for audit trails or integration with electronic medical records (EMRs).

    • Why use an LLM?
      Even for trained medical professionals, interpreting raw numerical logs alone can be challenging when assessing a patient’s condition. By leveraging a Large Language Model (LLM), we transform structured sensor data into intuitive, human-readable summaries that enhance situational awareness and clinical decision-making.
